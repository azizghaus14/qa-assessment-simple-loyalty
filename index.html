1. Assumptions

  • SMS credits are held per company (billing entity), not per brand. A brand may span several companies, so one submission can produce charges to more than one billing entity.
  • The commit point is the quotation: credits are only added once QuickBooks confirms the quotation was generated.
  • Emailing the quotation is treated as a separate, non-blocking step. If the email fails, the quotation and the credits stay valid and the email is retried, because reversing credits that the client has already been charged for would be worse than a delayed email.
  • Crediting across several companies happens in a single atomic transaction, so a partial top-up can never occur.
  • Each submission carries an idempotency key so a retry or double click cannot create a second quotation or double credits.

5. Highest-risk areas

  • Money and credits out of step (TC-09, TC-10, TC-12, TC-18, TC-19). The costly failure is a client being charged without credits, or credited twice. Idempotency and the atomic transaction are what prevent it.
  • Crediting the wrong entity (TC-21, TC-22, TC-23). The brand-to-company split is the trickiest rule in the feature, so it needs its own tests.
  • Silent partial success (TC-12, TC-14). The user must always be told exactly which of quotation, credits and email succeeded.
